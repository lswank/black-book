What it is

A drawer of smart helpers Claude can pull from.

You already talk to Claude. Black Book gives Claude eighty-six experts to call on — one for sales emails, one for the all-hands, one for the hiring decision. You don't pick which one. Claude does, silently, based on what you're asking. You just keep talking like always.

A drawer of helpers. Claude reaches in.

Three things people use it for

Real examples, not made-up ones.

The email you've been putting off

The one where you need to be firm without being rude. You've been staring at the blank reply for two days.

"Help me write a kind but firm email to a vendor who's missed three deadlines and keeps making excuses."

Black Book pulls in the cold-outreach helper. It asks what your last exchange looked like and what outcome you actually want — then writes the email. Three subject lines. The body. A kindly-worded close. You tweak a sentence. You send.

Two days of dread → five minutes of work.

The all-hands you have to lead

Rough quarter. Full team in the room tomorrow. You want to be honest without making everyone feel like the sky is falling.

"My team had a rough quarter. What should I say at the all-hands tomorrow?"

Black Book pulls in the leadership-comms helper. It asks what actually happened, who'll be there, and what you want them walking out feeling. Then it gives you structure, talking points, three openings to choose from. You pick the one that sounds like you.

A speech that lands, with five minutes of prep.

The decision you've been circling

The one without an obvious right answer. The one you keep rerunning in your head on walks.

"Should I hire a fifth person on my team this quarter, or wait?"

Black Book pulls in the team-leadership advisor. It asks what work is piling up, what your runway looks like, who you'd hire and why — then walks you through the trade-offs and points out the thing you hadn't been thinking about.

The call feels obvious. You move on.

Common questions

Things people ask first.

Is this safe to install?

Yes. Open source — read every line on GitHub. The downloaded file is about 4 KB; it just tells Claude where to find the eighty-six helpers. Your conversations stay between you and Claude.

Does it cost money?

No. Free, staying free. Soft cap of about thirty questions per hour on the shared server — plenty for normal use. Hit the cap and you can self-host; cap goes away.

Do I need to be technical to use it?

No. You keep talking to Claude like you always do. The install is two clicks.

What if Claude already knows about, say, marketing?

Generally, yes. Generally is the problem. The helpers are specific — they know what to ask you so the answer is actually useful. The difference shows up most when you're stuck.

What if I install it and don't like it?

Open Claude Desktop's settings, find Black Book, click remove. Five seconds. Nothing else changes.

What about Claude Code (the developer tool)?

There's a richer version with slash commands, sub-agents, and full-blown workflows. See the developers section below.

Who made this?

Lorenzo Swank made it. MIT licensed, on GitHub, free to fork and self-host.

Why this exists

A note from the maker.

Claude is a remarkable generalist. Most of us, though, aren't generalists at work — we have specific jobs, with specific problems, that have specific right answers.

Black Book is a small library of hand-tuned experts for the specific kind. It makes Claude noticeably better at office work without changing how you talk to it.

Free. Open source. Staying that way.
